## The current look, in short
- **Like your first three boss models:**
  - chunky faceted plates and blocks;
  - saturated red (about `#C8221E`) and charcoal grey (about `#3A3A3E`–`#6A6462`);
  - worn, scratched, slightly rusted metal and stone, not clean flat colour;
  - only a few glowing red-orange parts (visors, cracks, vents).
- **Crystal shards** like your card and plaque art: some red parts are faceted crystal (crowns, blades, spikes,
  lenses). They have sharp flat facets and bright edge highlights. The game adds the dark outline and glow, so don't
  paint them.
- **Keep the greys neutral.** Each biome tints enemies' grey parts: icy blue on Frozen Shore, violet on Starfall. The
  red and the glows never change.
- **The weak point glows gold.** It's shown by a gold core when the weak point opens. Model it as a recessed socket;
  the game adds the gold.
- **Readable from above:** the camera is fixed and isometric, about 35° down, and bosses are seen small. Silhouette
  matters more than surface detail.
- **No baked shadows or lighting in the texture.** The game lights everything: dark, warm fire, cool moonlight.

## Negative prompt (if the tool has one)

> text, letters, logo, watermark, background scenery, ground, base, pedestal, baked shadows, strong rim light, smooth
> organic skin, realistic photo, clean plastic look, blue or green colour scheme, gold armour, many tiny details

## How they go in

- **Delivery:** upload the `.glb` files and their textures to `unprocessed_images/` (or the repo root), named by id.
- **The lead's part:**
  - adds each file to `assets/models/manifest.json`;
  - sets its height and facing in `BossModels.SPECS` from a render of the raw model;
  - checks the asset test;
  - swaps the code-built body for your model.

  The fight doesn't change.
