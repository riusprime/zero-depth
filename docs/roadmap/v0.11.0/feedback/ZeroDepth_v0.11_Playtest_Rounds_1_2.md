# ZeroDepth v0.11 — Gameplay Feedback

Received from the owner 2026-11-09, kept verbatim. Where it lands: [`../PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) "Playtest rounds 1 & 2".

**Playtest feedback — Rounds 1 & 2**

## 1. Enemies — Models and Visibility

### 1.1 New enemy models are too dark
- The newly introduced enemies that already have 3D models generally fit the game's visual style well.
- However, some of these models appear slightly too dark.
- They should be a little brighter without significantly changing their appearance or making them excessively illuminated.

### 1.2 Small enemy hordes blend into the environment
- The new hordes of small enemies work very well from a gameplay perspective.
- However, their visibility is a problem in certain biomes.
- Tested in Ruins and Frozen Shore, where their colors are too similar to the environment.
- These enemies become difficult to distinguish from the floor during combat.
- Their readability against different biome backgrounds needs improvement.

### 1.3 Purple projectile caster — Missing model
- There is an older purple enemy that places a circle on the ground and launches three projectiles.
- It is one of the early-game enemies and still lacks a proper model.
- Identify the enemy's existing name in the project.
- Consider reusing the existing Priest model with a modified color palette, since the two enemies have similar appearances.
- Reusing this model would also avoid additional AI model-generation costs.

## 2. Enemy Mechanics and Effects

### 2.1 Rock-producing enemy
- The large enemy that leaves rocks on the ground does not currently feel right.
- It can leave approximately eight rocks behind, creating obstacles that prevent other enemies from moving through the area.
- This leads to strange moments during fights and negatively affects enemy movement.
- The rocks also do not react to the white hit-flash animation, making them feel visually disconnected from other damageable enemies or objects.
- The entire rock mechanic should be reconsidered, especially the accumulation of persistent obstacles.

### 2.2 Small exploding enemies
- Their explosion visual is disproportionately large compared to the small enemy size.
- The explosion should be noticeably smaller.
- The explosion animation also sometimes plays two or three times instead of once.
- Each explosion should happen only once.

## 3. Bosses — Twin Wardens

### 3.1 Pillar mechanic
The pillars look good visually, but the mechanic currently has too little impact and happens too quickly.

Requested changes:

- Increase the earthquake/damage area generated by the pillars so they become a meaningful threat to the player.
- The player should not be able to simply ignore the pillars.
- Replace the obvious instructional text appearing with the pillars with simpler directional arrows pointing toward them.
- Increase the duration of the cross-shaped ground marker beneath the player to approximately **2 seconds** before the ground attack activates.
- This gives the player enough time to position the attack toward a pillar.
- Increase the boss's stun duration after successfully executing the mechanic. Currently the stun feels too short to reward the interaction properly.

**Desired encounter flow:**

1. Pillars appear, clearly indicated without excessive text.
2. Pillars generate a meaningful damaging threat.
3. The player receives the ground-cross targeting marker.
4. The player has approximately 2 seconds to position themselves and direct the attack.
5. The ground attack activates and can interact with a pillar.
6. Successful execution stuns the boss for a meaningful duration.

The intention is to make this interaction more readable, deliberate, and satisfying rather than rushed.

## 4. Boss Arenas — Reconsider Shrinking Rooms

### 4.1 Current shrinking arena mechanic
- The current mechanic that progressively reduces boss arena size using red effects extending from the walls is not particularly enjoyable.
- It also feels repetitive when used across different boss fights.
- Boss arenas should retain their original boundaries rather than continuously shrinking in the same way.

### 4.2 Alternative: Persistent environmental hazards

For selected bosses, use environmental hazards that progressively occupy parts of the arena instead of shrinking the entire room.

Possible examples:

- Lava pools that remain on the ground.
- Venom pools that reduce safe movement areas.
- Falling rocks that create temporary dangerous or slowing zones.
- Other boss-specific environmental hazards.

These hazards should accumulate during the fight but have a strict maximum cap, preventing the entire arena from becoming unusable.

### 4.3 Warlord — Returning javelin concept
- The Warlord throws a javelin into the arena.
- The javelin remains embedded in the ground and generates an earthquake-like effect, similar to the existing rock-based attack.
- It vibrates for a period before the boss recalls it.
- On its return path toward the boss, the javelin can damage the player.

### 4.4 Boss-specific exceptions

Not every boss should use accumulating environmental hazards.

- **Lantern boss:** Keep the existing darkness and light-pillar mechanic. Navigating darkness and restoring visibility is already the encounter's defining feature.
- **Twin Wardens:** Do not add these accumulating environmental hazards. Their encounter already has its own specific mechanics.
- **Hollow Crown:** Do not add this mechanic either.

The environmental hazards should provide variety for appropriate bosses rather than become another universal mechanic.

## 5. UI — Reduce Text and Visual Clutter

### 5.1 Build management interface
The build interface currently contains too much instructional and statistical information.

Examples include:
- Forecast descriptions.
- Damage-per-second information.
- Shots per attack.
- Damage per shot.
- Instructions for picking up, placing, moving, and swapping cards across mouse/controller inputs.

Requested changes:

- Remove unnecessary instructions explaining basic card interactions.
- Make card movement and swapping visually clearer through animations.
- When moving a card, it should be immediately obvious which card is selected and where it is being moved.
- Remove the detailed **shots per attack, damage per shot, and DPS** text from the relevant interface.
- Make the forecast more visual instead of relying on several lines of text.

### 5.2 Difficulty and depth information
- The current interface displays information such as the depth multiplier (for example, ×1.41), build strength, and explanatory text about enemy difficulty.
- This relies too heavily on written descriptions.
- Keep useful information, such as the depth multiplier, but represent the difficulty more visually.
- Consider skulls or another symbol that clearly communicates difficulty.
- The player should be able to understand the difficulty level at a glance.

### 5.3 Pickup and combination notifications
- Some card or reward notifications appear too prominently near the center of the screen.
- They can obstruct gameplay and hide enemies or other important elements.
- Move these notifications to a smaller, thinner area in the upper-right portion of the screen, beneath the existing horde indicator.
- Shorten the descriptions.
- Elemental combination notifications should be especially compact and visually recognizable.
- Prioritize symbols and short information rather than large explanatory cards.

### 5.4 Shrine reward animations
The shrine currently creates large reward notifications in the middle of the screen even though the obtained buffs are already represented in the corner UI.

Desired interaction:

1. The player activates the shrine.
2. Different reward symbols appear above the shrine, cycling as if they are being rolled.
3. The selected reward symbol stops on the final result.
4. The symbol moves toward the existing reward/buff display in the upper-right corner.
5. The existing buff display updates.

Remove the large central reward notification.

The goal is to communicate the reward without interrupting gameplay or obscuring the screen.

## 6. Healing — Major Balance Rework

### 6.1 Current problems
- Healing is currently too powerful, particularly when fighting large numbers of weaker enemies.
- Killing adds can generate large quantities of green healing orbs.
- Seven or eight healing orbs can accumulate on the ground even when the player is already at full health.
- Players can effectively stockpile healing, take hits, and immediately recover.
- Life Sprout contributes to the excessive availability of health recovery.

### 6.2 Remove Life Sprout
- Temporarily disable the Life Sprout card.
- Keep kill-based healing as a default gameplay mechanic instead of requiring this card.
- Healing should still be available through combat, but significantly more controlled.

### 6.3 Desired healing philosophy
- The player should be able to recover health through continued combat.
- Healing should reward surviving and killing enemies.
- The game should not become a no-hit challenge where taking damage is excessively punishing.
- However, players should not be able to recover health faster than enemies can realistically threaten them.
- Healing orbs should not accumulate into large reserves.

### 6.4 Proposed baseline rules for balancing

These numbers are an initial balance target, not final values.

| Parameter | Initial target |
|---|---|
| Life Sprout | Disabled |
| Kill-based healing | Default mechanic |
| Healing orb recovery | 20% of maximum HP |
| Healing cooldown | 30 seconds between successful pickups |
| Maximum healing rate | 40% maximum HP per minute |
| Orbs simultaneously available on the ground | 1 |
| Healing progress | Earned by killing enemies |
| Expected recovery from nearly empty to full HP | Approximately 2–3 minutes of sustained combat |

**Generation and pickup rules:**

- Enemy kills contribute toward spawning a healing orb.
- Only one healing orb may exist on the ground at any given time.
- At full health, the player may have one healing orb available, but cannot accumulate additional ones.
- Picking up a healing orb starts a 30-second healing cooldown.
- During that cooldown, no additional healing orb should become available.
- Once the cooldown ends, the player must continue killing enemies to earn another heal.
- The required kills/progress must be balanced so that different enemy quantities do not generate excessive healing.

**Balance considerations:**

- Five pickups restoring 20% maximum HP each would provide a full health bar.
- With a 30-second cooldown, the theoretical recovery time is around 2–3 minutes, depending on how quickly kills generate orbs.
- This limits healing availability, removes large orb reserves, and makes repeated damage more consequential.
- Values should be evaluated during actual gameplay, especially against large enemy hordes and in encounters with fewer enemies.

The key objective is **controlled, consistent healing that supports normal combat without enabling effectively unlimited health regeneration.**

## 7. Hive Lens — Beam Attacks

- The Hive Lens has two types of beam attacks.
- Both move very quickly.
- Their tracking behavior makes them extremely difficult to dodge because they follow the player so aggressively.
- The attacks should feel more like conventional, readable boss spells with a reasonable dodge opportunity.
- Review the speed and tracking behavior of both beam variants.

---

## Overall Feedback Direction

The main priorities emerging from this playtest are:

1. Improve enemy visibility without significantly changing their designs.
2. Reduce visual clutter, oversized effects, and repeated animation issues.
3. Make boss mechanics clearer and more rewarding.
4. Differentiate boss encounters instead of reusing shrinking-arena mechanics.
5. Make the UI more visual and less dependent on instructional text.
6. Prevent excessive healing while preserving a reliable way to recover health during combat.

**Scope:** Consolidated feedback from v0.11 playtest rounds 1 and 2. Existing implementation details, unconfirmed enemy names, and final numerical balance decisions remain with the development agent.
